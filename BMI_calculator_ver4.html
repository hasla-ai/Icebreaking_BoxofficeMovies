01 / Read

Sequencing & genotyping

Sequencing reads the order of DNA letters; genotyping checks selected known variants. These methods create molecular data that researchers can analyze.

Keep in mind

A measurement is not a diagnosis. Results need quality checks and interpretation for the specific person and question.

How responsible discovery works

From biological data
to a careful question.

AI can support steps in research. It does not turn a BMI result into a genome, a diagnosis, or a complete forecast.

STEP 01

Collect

Use an appropriate sample or measurement with clear consent and purpose.

STEP 02

Check

Review data quality, missingness, and whether the dataset fits the question.

STEP 03

Model

Use statistics or AI to find patterns and rank hypotheses.

STEP 04

Validate

Test findings in appropriate samples, settings, and populations.

STEP 05

Interpret

Combine evidence with clinical context and expert judgment.

✳
Prediction is not certainty. Research models can reflect gaps or bias in the data used to build them.
03 — The honest boundary

More data can help.
It still has limits.

Health is shaped by biology, environment, time, and chance. No single score or AI system captures every cause of disease or pain.

A better question

What evidence
do we actually have?

Good science states what was measured, who the evidence applies to, how uncertain it is, and what would need follow-up.

Not a crystal ball · A map of evidence
01 / BODY

Same BMI, different bodies

Muscle, bone, fat distribution, age, and other factors are not visible in the BMI number.

02 / DNA

Genes are not destiny

Many common traits involve many variants and non-genetic influences. A score gives a limited statistical view.

03 / AI

Patterns need proof

A model may find a signal in its training data. Independent validation is needed before real-world use.

04 / PRIVACY

DNA is personal data

Genomic information can have implications for relatives too. Consent, access, storage, and sharing matter.

Quick explainer · Local content

Can BMI explain a pain?

No. BMI is a height-and-weight calculation, not a pain assessment. Pain can have many causes. A new, severe, persistent, or worrying symptom needs assessment based on the symptom and the person’s circumstances.
